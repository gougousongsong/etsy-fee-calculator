The deposit is $71.04, but $4.60 of that is postage you owe the carrier. If the buyer paid $4.00 for shipping and the label costs $4.60, the shipping line is losing you $0.60 on every order — before Etsy's cut is counted at all.

Why $75 is a specific kind of price

At $75 the fees are almost entirely percentage-based: $0.45 of flat fees is 0.6% of the order. That inverts the problem — the risk moves from Etsy's fee schedule to the cost of the goods and the chance of a return.

It is also the price band where free shipping usually pays. Absorbing $4.60 of postage into a $79 price costs 5.8% of the order, while the same postage charged separately would cost 9.5% in fees on the shipping line.

What to do about it

  • Absorb shipping into the price. On high-ticket items free shipping is cheaper than charging for it, because the fee only applies to what you charge.
  • Insure the parcel. At this order value a lost package costs more than the insurance ever will.
  • Check the break-even. Profitable up to $66.44 of materials and packaging; above that you are working for free.
  • Price the ad in. An offsite ad sale still deposits $59.19, so a $75 product survives the 15% far better than a $15 one.

Frequently asked

How much does Etsy take on a $75 sale?

On a $75.00 item with $4.00 shipping, a US seller pays $7.96 in total fees: $5.14 transaction fee, $2.62 payment processing and $0.20 listing. That is 10.1% of the $79.00 the buyer pays, leaving a $71.04 deposit.

Is a $75 item worth selling on Etsy?

Yes. Flat fees shrink to 0.6% of the order here, so almost all of Etsy's cut is proportional. The real risks at this price are material cost, returns and damage in transit — not the fee schedule.

Does charging shipping separately reduce Etsy fees?

No. The 6.5% transaction fee and the 3% processing fee both apply to item price plus shipping, so the total fee is identical whether the money sits in the price or in the shipping line.

What happens if an offsite ad is attributed to the sale?

Etsy adds 15% of the order (12% for shops over $10,000 a year), capped at $100. On this order that is an extra $11.85, which cuts the deposit from $71.04 to $59.19.
