The four costs that quietly kill a product

  • Ad spend per unit. A product that only sells on ads has to carry the ad cost in every unit, not only in the launch month.
  • Returns. An 8% return rate with a $12 loss per return costs about $0.96 on every unit shipped, not only on the returned ones.
  • Storage time. Storage is charged per cubic foot per month. Slow stock costs more the longer it sits, which is why "months of storage" is an input rather than a constant.
  • Inbound freight and prep. Both are real cash per unit and both are invisible in a referral-fee calculation.

Frequently asked

Is the referral fee charged on the sale price or the profit?

On the total the buyer pays — item price plus any delivery or gift wrap, excluding tax. Selling at a higher price also raises the referral fee, which is why "just charge more" is not free.

Why does my storage cost look tiny but my profit still disappears?

Storage is rarely the killer on its own. Ads, returns and inbound freight usually are. Change the ad spend to zero and see how much of the profit was actually the ad budget.

Does this include the $39.99 professional selling plan?

No — that is an account-level cost, not a per-unit one. Divide it by your monthly volume and add it to product cost if you want it in the picture.
