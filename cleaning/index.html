Why the hourly rate you quote is not your wage

If one cleaner costs $18 an hour and the job takes three hours, the job costs $54 before supplies, before the van, before insurance and before the owner earns anything. Add 0.5 hours of unpaid travel per cleaner and the real cost of that "three hour" job is already $63. Quoting $25 an hour because a competitor did is how cleaning businesses end up busy and broke.

The number that matters is the effective rate per on-site hour in the table: the price divided by the hours the customer can see. If that number is close to your wage rate, the business is not making money — it is buying itself a job.

What this calculator does not include

  • Owner's own salary when the owner also cleans — treat it as a labour cost or the margin is fictional.
  • Income tax, sales tax or VAT you have to collect and remit.
  • Equipment replacement cycles (vacuums, extractors) beyond the monthly fixed cost you enter.
  • Cancellations and drive-bys, which is why the jobs-per-month figure should be realistic rather than best case.

Frequently asked

Should travel time be charged to the customer?

It has to be paid for by somebody. Either it sits inside the price as a cost (what this calculator does) or you add a travel fee, but leaving it out entirely means every job quietly loses money.

What margin is normal for cleaning?

There is no universal number, and anyone quoting one without knowing the market is guessing. Enter the margin you need to cover owner pay and reinvestment, then check whether the resulting price is one your local market actually accepts. If it is not, the answer is scope or frequency, not a thinner margin.

Why does my allocated fixed cost look high?

Because it is spread over the jobs you enter. Fewer jobs per month means each one has to carry more of the van, insurance and software. That is exactly the trap small operators fall into when they price for a full calendar they do not have.
