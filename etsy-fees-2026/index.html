How much does Etsy take in 2026?

Short answer: about 10% of the order. Longer answer: the percentage is the part everyone knows, and the flat fees plus the fees nobody mentions are the part that decides whether your shop makes money.

Worked examples, US seller, no ads

These are before your materials, packaging, labour and any advertising. The percentage you keep rises with order value because the $0.25 processing component and the $0.20 listing fee stop scaling.

The four fees that make up the bill

1. Transaction fee — 6.5%

Charged on the total the buyer pays: item price, shipping and gift wrap. Not on your profit, and not on the item price alone. A $35 item with $5 shipping pays 6.5% of $40, which is $2.60.

2. Payment processing — 3% + $0.25 in the US

Charged on the same total, and it is the fee that punishes cheap items. On a $4 sticker that $0.25 alone is 6% of the sale before the percentage even applies.

3. Listing fee — $0.20

Charged when you publish, and again every time the listing auto-renews after four months or after a sale. Sellers with a large catalogue pay this constantly and almost never count it.

4. Offsite ads — 15%, or 12% above $10,000 a year

Only on orders Etsy attributes to an offsite ad click, capped at $100 per order. Optional below the annual threshold and mandatory above it. This is the fee that turns a profitable product into a loss, because it stacks on top of the first three.

The fees people forget, and why they matter more than the 6.5%

  • Shipping is fee-bearing. Raising shipping and lowering item price changes nothing for Etsy, but raising the item price and offering "free" shipping changes nothing either — the same 6.5% applies to the same money.
  • Currency conversion, 2.5%. If your shop currency differs from the currency the buyer pays in, Etsy charges it on the converted amount. Sellers in non-USD shops feel this on every international order.
  • Regional operating fees. Some countries add a percentage on top of the standard stack. Check your own region rather than assuming the US rate applies.
  • Etsy Ads are not a fee, they are a cost. Budget spend comes out of your revenue whether or not the ads produce a sale, which is why it belongs in the product cost, not in the fee line.
  • Refunds are asymmetric. Etsy refunds the transaction fee on a cancelled order but you carry the processing component and the listing fee, so a cancelled sale still costs you money.

What this means for pricing

Fixed costs per order — the $0.25 processing component and the $0.20 listing fee — are why cheap items are a trap on Etsy. On a $6 item they consume 7.5% of the price before the percentage fees start. Two things follow:

  • Under roughly $10, raise the price or bundle, because the flat fees dominate the margin.
  • Above roughly $40, the flat fees stop mattering and advertising becomes the variable that decides profitability.

The break-even price is the number worth knowing: the price at which you make exactly nothing. Work it out once per product and you stop guessing which listings are actually losing money.

Frequently asked

Does Etsy take 6.5% of shipping?

Yes. The transaction fee applies to item price plus shipping plus gift wrap. This is the single most common surprise for new sellers.

Is there a fee when I list an item, or only when it sells?

Both. You pay $0.20 to publish and $0.20 again on auto-renewal, whether or not the item sells. A listing that sits unsold for a year costs $0.60 in listing fees alone.

Do the fees change if I sell digital products?

The transaction and processing fees are the same. There is no shipping, so the fee base is smaller, but the $0.20 listing fee matters more relative to a low-priced digital file.

What is the actual percentage Etsy takes overall?

On a typical US order without advertising it lands between 10% and 11.5% of the total the buyer pays, depending on order size, because the flat fees weigh more on small orders. With offsite ads attributed to the sale, add 15% (or 12%) of the order.
