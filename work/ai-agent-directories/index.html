I fetched all 40 “AI agent directories”. 28 of them publish no email address.

Probe date 2026-11-27 · 40 of 40 rows checked · data is downloadable, no signup

Every “40 AI agent directories to submit your agent to” post I could find is copy-pasted, and most of them list a contact email for each directory. I needed that list for a real submission round, so instead of trusting it I fetched all 40 pages with a desktop user agent, followed redirects, and recorded what the page actually showed. Here is what came back.

Why the emails matter

A list that gives you an address for all 40 rows is not being helpful, it is filling gaps with hello@domain.com. When I checked the guessed aliases against MX records, a good share did not resolve to anything that could receive mail. If you send an outreach batch to those, you learn nothing except that a made-up address bounces.

So the CSV has exactly two possible values in the contact column: an address that was printed on the site and whose domain has live MX, or the literal string web form only. No third option, no guesses.

The 10 Cloudflare 403s are not dead

This is the trap in automated list-building. A script gets 403, the row gets marked “broken”, and a working directory is dropped. Those 10 pages render fine in a real browser — they just refuse python-requests. They are kept in the CSV with their HTTP status so you can retry them the slow way instead of deleting them.

Pricing is a signal, not a quote

A price is only recorded when a currency amount appears within about 120 characters of a submission/listing/fee word. That is deliberately conservative: several of those ranges include tier or template prices. Treat the number as “go look at this page”, not as a quote. Where nothing was stated, the row says not stated on page — which, for 19 of 40 directories, is also a finding: they either review for free or want you to ask.
